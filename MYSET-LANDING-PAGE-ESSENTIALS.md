# MySet landing page — essentials only

**Core diagnosis:** The page makes MySet easy to understand and easy to try, but not yet easy enough to believe. The copy is strong; the proof, focus, promise integrity, and signup handoff are the real bottlenecks.

## Fix these first

1. **Add real proof near the top.**  
   Use one named artist, venue, quote, and verified gig result. Screenshots prove that software exists; they do not prove that a gig improves.

2. **Keep the artist as the only hero.**  
   Move the full venue pitch to a dedicated page. On the artist page, use one short link: “See MySet for venues.”

3. **State that the artist stays in control.**  
   Add near the hero: “You choose which songs the room can see—and you always decide what fits.” This neutralizes the biggest likely objection.

4. **Fix promises that exceed availability.**  
   Tips and paid votes are described as normal features before the page says they are enabled artist by artist. Pro features are said to be “marked” as forthcoming, but they are not visibly marked. Label availability at the first mention or remove the claims for now.

5. **Fix the CTA handoff.**  
   “Get your page — free” currently lands on “Sign in to run your show,” which sounds like a returning-user page. Change it to “Create or open your artist page” with a “Continue with email” button.

6. **Show a success plan, not signup mechanics.**  
   Replace “email → code → name” with:
   1. Choose the songs the room can see.
   2. Display your QR.
   3. See what they want, play what fits, and keep the results.

7. **Instrument the full funnel before redesigning.**  
   Measure: landing view → CTA → email → code verified → setlist → QR → first show → first audience vote → second show. Optimize for activated artists, not clicks.

## Keep

- The core headline as an A/B-test control.
- The “no app, account, or download” promise.
- The live voting demo.
- The vivid “guessing between songs” story.
- Free/no-card risk reversal.
- The candid, human tone.

## Simplify

The mobile page is roughly 22 screens long. Compress the five screenshot-heavy feature sections into three outcomes:

- **Calm:** stop guessing between songs.
- **Energy:** the room becomes invested in the winner.
- **Proof:** leave with evidence for future bookings.

## Recommended hero test

> **For live musicians who are tired of guessing**
>
> **Turn a distracted room into a crowd that chooses the next song.**
>
> Everyone votes from their phone—no app or account. You choose which songs they can see, decide what fits, and leave with proof of what the room wanted.
>
> **Create my free artist page** · **Try a live vote**
>
> Free forever · no card · set up before your next gig

## Priestley application

Do **not** force a 15-question assessment before free signup. Keep direct product activation for high-intent visitors. Build a separate “Live Set Engagement Score” funnel for cold traffic to measure Room Energy, Show Flow, and Booking Proof, then route people to self-serve signup, guided setup, or nurture based on readiness.

## Best order of work

1. Measurement.
2. Promise/availability corrections.
3. Signup handoff.
4. Artist-control line.
5. Named proof.
6. Artist/venue split.
7. Shorter page and copy test.

**If only one thing changes:** put a credible, named real-gig result directly under the hero. The page already explains the product; it now needs to prove the outcome.
