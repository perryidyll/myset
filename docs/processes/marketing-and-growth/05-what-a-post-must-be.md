# What a post must be — channels, hooks, franchises

**Who:** the founder, and whoever edits. **Trigger:** a brief exists (Automation engine a03, or by hand) and a post is about to be made. **Outcome:** the post is on the right channel for its job, cut to that channel's doctrine, opens on a hook from a family with evidence, shows a live-performance signal in the frame, belongs to a franchise with a fixed name and treatment, dramatises an objection instead of walking through a feature, and is posted as a collaboration wherever an artist is in it. These are rules, not a sequence: every post passes all of them.

All **Draft** — the accounts do not exist yet (Appendix C).

| n01 | Choose the channel by its role | task | Person | Founder R | — | Instagram Reels — primary reach and the ICP's habitat, priority 1. TikTok — secondary reach and format R&D. YouTube Shorts — overflow of the same vertical asset. YouTube long-form and search — compounding authority, rising to priority 1 over a year. Facebook Groups — direct ICP access, participate genuinely, **never link-drop**. Email — the only owned audience. The venue circuit — Scene Zero. Reddit for listening; X/Threads for build-in-public; LinkedIn later, for venues. `src: strategy §6.1` |
| n02 | Cut for the platform | video | Person | Founder R | — | Instagram: 9:16; seven to thirty seconds for Attract, thirty to sixty for Position, sixty to ninety for Nurture; the hook — the choice, the stake or the tension — inside the first 1.5 seconds, on screen **and** spoken; real gig audio wherever possible (crowd noise is a credibility signal; trending audio only for Chaos tests); captions burned in, high contrast, above the UI safe zone. TikTok: faster, rawer; **stitch and duet bait is the core mechanic** — post a setlist dilemma and invite stitches; the Series feature for franchises. `src: strategy §6.2, §6.3` |
| n03 | Put a live signal in every Attract frame | task | Person | Founder R | — | A stage, an instrument, a venue, gig audio, a performer's POV, or the MySet tally — **never a bare graphic**. A text poll asking "which song saves a dead bar?" attracts fans; a working musician on a real stage in bad light asking the same thing attracts musicians *and* fans, and converts the musicians. Costs nothing, roughly doubles the ICP density of the reach. `src: strategy §7.4` |
| n04 | Write the hook from a family | document | Person | Founder R | — | Context + stakes + choice in the first second, from one of the nine families — Constraint, Rescue, Betrayal, Elimination, Prediction, Contradiction, Confession, Insider, Data — with the templates and examples in §7.5. **Banned:** "Favourite song?", "Comment below!", "Let me know what you think" — low-stakes prompts teach the algorithm the audience is passive. The generator may only use families with logged evidence, or route a new one to the Chaos slot (Automation engine a10). `src: strategy §7.5, §9.4` |
| n05 | Dramatise the objection, never walk through the feature | task | Person | Founder R · Artist C | — | Each objection becomes a **situation**, with the mechanism revealed second: *"my crowd won't download anything"* → film a stranger scanning and voting in under eight seconds; *"I don't want the crowd running my gig"* → reject a voted song on camera; *"what if the Wi-Fi dies?"* → the literal worst case, handled; *"I already read rooms fine"* → predict five songs and get three wrong — the only content that makes a confident performer feel a gap, and **confidence is the real objection**. The full matrix is §7.8. `src: strategy §7.8` |
| n06 | Keep the franchise fixed | task | Person | Founder R | — | A franchise has a fixed name, a fixed opening frame, a fixed visual treatment and a fixed length — recognition is the entire point. Launch order by leverage: SAVE THE GIG, READ THE ROOM and BUILDING MYSET in week one; ONE HAS TO GO week two; ARTIST vs AUDIENCE week three (needs one artist); ROOM REPORT and REQUEST DENIED week four (need gigs); TRY THIS TONIGHT week five (Stage B only); THE PERFECT SET and WHAT WON? week six; CROWD CONTROL at Scene One. Briefs in Appendix B. `src: strategy §7.7, Appendix B` |
| n07 | Post from the LIBRARY | video | Person | Founder R | — | Short video is a slot machine; search is an annuity. One long-form piece a fortnight, ten to eighteen minutes, on a query cluster the ICP actually types — repertoire, set construction, crowd management, **business** (*"how to get rebooked at a bar"* — the highest value, least served, and MySet's answer is differentiated by the Room Report), gear. Each long-form yields four to six Shorts. Search volumes are **unverified** — validate with a keyword tool before spending production time. `src: strategy §6.4` |
| n08 | Post as a collaboration | task | Person | Founder R · Artist R | — | Every post with a Founding 50 artist in it goes out as a collab post with them — their followers are hundreds to thousands of exactly the right people, and it costs nothing. The single highest-leverage growth mechanic available. Collab posts are posted by hand, not scheduled (Automation engine a06). `src: strategy §6.2 collab posts, §6.7 #1` |
| n09 | Borrow a creator's audience | chat | Person | Founder R | — | Ten to fifteen gigging-musician creators in the five-thousand to hundred-thousand range. **Do not pitch a sponsorship; pitch a format**: *"let your audience pick your next set, on camera, and we will build it"* — a good video for them free, a perfect demo for MySet. REQUEST DENIED is the natural collab franchise. `src: strategy §6.7 #2, §7.7 #8` |
| n10 | Feed the owned list | email | Person | Founder R · Artist I | Resend | **Draft — no capture exists.** Lead magnets in expected order: the Crowd-Pleaser 100 (segmented by venue type and decade, on-ramps to "import these into MySet"), the Dead Room Playbook, the Venue Pitch Kit. Then five emails over ten days: useful, useful, story, soft offer, Founding 50 invitation. The list and the moat are the same asset class. Sending would run through Resend, which is already MySet's mail (→ *Reliability & security → Secrets and keys*); a list product is not chosen. `src: strategy §6.5` |
| n11 | Keep two accounts, both with a face | task | Person | Founder R | — | Dual-account, founder-led: a **new** music-specific founder account, separate from the founder's existing personal wellness identity (mixing the two audiences damages both and confuses the algorithm's categorisation), and a brand account. The brand account posts everything; the founder account posts the human half and is the face in brand content. If capacity forces one: the brand account with the founder's face in it constantly — **never a faceless brand account**. Expensive to reverse, so decided before day one. `src: strategy §6.6, Appendix C` |
