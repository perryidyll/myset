## 1. Executive summary

Strategy 2.0 is a genuinely good **content** strategy. It is not yet a **go-to-market** strategy, and MySet's bottleneck right now is go-to-market, not content ideas.

The single most important fact in the entire handover is this: MySet has one real gig of evidence, eight voters, twenty-one votes, one three-dollar purchase. A company at that stage does not have a content problem. It has a supply problem. It needs working musicians running real shows, because those shows are simultaneously the product validation, the proof, the dataset, and the raw footage that makes every other part of the plan possible.

Strategy 2.0 writes a twenty-eight-post calendar that structurally depends on gig footage that does not exist and cannot exist until artists are recruited. It builds the marketing engine before building the fuel line.

**Version 3.0 makes seven structural corrections:**

1. **Sequence inverted.** Recruit artists first, generate gigs second, produce content third. The first thirty days are an outbound recruitment sprint dressed as a content launch, not the reverse.
2. **The buyer promise is repositioned around money, not magic.** "Turn the room into part of the show" is a beautiful brand line and a weak sales line. The working musician's actual problem is getting rebooked and raising their rate. MySet's commercial promise is: *the room votes, the venue rebooks.* Engagement data is the artist's leverage with venue managers. This is the missing conversion argument.
3. **A distribution strategy is added.** Strategy 2.0 never names a platform. Platform physics differ enormously and a seven-day cadence is meaningless without them. §6 specifies primary, secondary, and experimental channels with native formats and cadence.
4. **An offer is added.** "Try it at your next gig" is a call to action with no offer behind it. The gap between watching a Reel and importing 150 songs is enormous. §5 builds the Founding 50 programme, the friction demolition, and the concierge onboarding that makes activation actually happen.
5. **Geography is moved from Phase 3 to Phase 1.** Ten performers across ten countries reinforce nothing. Density is the mechanism, not a later bonus. Scene Zero is Koh Phangan, chosen because you are physically there and can shoot. Scene One is a Western market chosen on criteria in §10.
6. **Funnel weighting becomes dynamic.** Jun Yuh's 3 Attract / 1 Nurture / 2 Position / 1 Convert is a creator ratio for an established creator. A pre-proof company should overweight Journey early and Convert late. §7.3 specifies three weightings by stage.
7. **Search is added as a compounding channel.** Working musicians actively search for "best bar gig songs," "how to handle requests," "cover songs that always work." That is high-intent, permanently indexed demand aimed directly at the ICP, and it is completely absent from Strategy 2.0. §6.4.

Everything else in Strategy 2.0 that is good has been kept and, in several places, sharpened. The intellectual core of that document, that content should behave like the product and that evidence should never be fabricated, is correct and load-bearing. Do not lose it.

## 2. Strategic audit of Strategy 2.0

### 2.1 What is genuinely strong and must be kept

| Idea | Why it holds |
|---|---|
| Cross the two axes rather than blend them | Topic buckets and funnel jobs are orthogonal. Crossing them gives seven distinct briefs instead of seven vague ones. Structurally correct and automation-friendly. |
| Broad-but-adjacent hooks | The best single idea in the document. "Best '90s song" builds a fan audience with zero purchase intent. "Best '90s song to rescue a dead bar crowd" builds the same reach while training the algorithm and the viewer toward live performance. Keep this rule absolutely. |
| Evidence integrity rule | Refusing to let aspirational template copy masquerade as results is both ethically right and strategically smart. It converts earliness into a Journey narrative instead of a credibility bomb waiting to go off. |
| The content itself should behave like the product | Every Attract post is a miniature gig. This is the deepest insight across both prior documents. |
| The scoreboard as visual grammar | The tally is a genuine visual asset. Sports, elections, and game shows have taught audiences to read a live-updating ranked list. Free comprehension. |
| One gig produces a content packet, not a Reel | Correct multiplier thinking. §8 expands it into an actual production system. |
| North Star is activated gigs, not followers | Right metric. §12 adds the attribution mechanics that make it measurable. |
| Kill formats faster than functions | Subtle and correct. Read The Room will always lose on views to You Pick The Set, and killing it on that basis would turn MySet into an entertainment account. |
| Crowd Lab over Crowd Controlled | Correct read of performer psychology. Musicians do not want to advertise that strangers run their gig. |

### 2.2 What is missing, and what it costs

| Gap | Consequence if unfixed | Fixed in |
|---|---|---|
| **No platform strategy** | The calendar is unshootable. Vertical short video, long-form YouTube, and Facebook Groups need different assets, different lengths, different hooks. | §6 |
| **No production capacity model** | Seven posts a week from a solo founder who is also building the product and running two other ventures fails in week three. There is no batch model, no minimum viable cadence, no fallback. | §8.4, §15 |
| **No cold-start plan** | The document assumes distribution. A zero-follower account posting excellent content into a void is still a void. There is no borrowed-audience play, no creator collaboration, no community infiltration, no outbound. | §10 |
| **No offer** | Convert content points at a free signup with brutal onboarding friction. Import 150 songs, build a setlist, print a QR, all before a gig where failure is embarrassing in front of a paying venue. Nobody crosses that gap from a Reel. | §5 |
| **No owned audience** | Everything sits on rented land. The twenty artists who nearly signed up are unreachable forever. | §6.5 |
| **No direct sales motion** | The realistic reachable ICP in year one is small and individually identifiable. Every gigging musician who posts gig footage publicly is a named lead. DM outreach will outperform organic reach by an order of magnitude for the first hundred activations. Social should support outbound, not substitute for it. | §10.2 |
| **Attract still routes to fans** | Broad-but-adjacent fixes the topic. It does not fix the fact that a faceless poll graphic attracts fans. Format-level qualification beats topic-level qualification: a visibly working musician on a real stage filters the audience far harder than the wording of the question. | §7.4 |
| **No face or account architecture** | Faceless brand accounts underperform badly. Nobody is specified as the on-camera human, and the founder-account versus brand-account decision is never made. | §6.6 |
| **Geography deferred** | Density is treated as a Phase 3 reward. It is actually the Phase 1 mechanism, and the only way a solo founder can physically produce gig footage. | §10.3 |
| **No willingness-to-pay analysis** | Musicians are famously reluctant software buyers. Ten dollars a month needs an ROI story. The document never builds one. | §3.4, §4.4 |
| **Nurture underweighted at stage zero** | Journey and Nurture are the only honest content producible with no users, and build-in-public is precisely what recruits early-adopter musicians. Fixing the ratio at 3/1/2/1 from day one is wrong. | §7.3 |
| **Comment sections unmanaged** | For participation content, the comment section is the product demo and the primary distribution mechanism. No strategy for pinned comments, reply-videos, or the second-post reveal loop. | §7.6 |
| **No hook library or swipe operationalisation** | "Analyse outliers" with nowhere to store the output means the learning evaporates. | §9.4 |
| **No automation architecture** | You explicitly want an automated engine. Strategy 2.0 has no schema, no pipeline, no agent roles, no human-in-the-loop gates. | §9 |
| **Calendar depends on nonexistent footage** | Week 2 Saturday requires a real gig prediction. Week 3 Saturday requires its reveal. Neither can be shot until artists exist. | §11 |

### 2.3 The one place Strategy 2.0 contradicts itself

It argues, correctly and at length, that the account must narrow hard onto working performers to build authority density. It then allocates three of seven weekly posts to broad music-fandom participation content whose primary audience is fans.

That tension is resolvable and the document half-resolves it with broad-but-adjacent. But the full resolution requires accepting a trade the document never names: **the Attract bucket is not primarily an acquisition channel for buyers. It is an acquisition channel for the algorithm and for social proof.** Its job is to make the account look alive and give the platform enough engagement signal to distribute the narrow content. Buyers mostly come from Position, Convert, search, and outbound.

Naming that honestly changes how you measure Attract. See §12.3.

## 3. Positioning: the corrected stack

### 3.1 What stays

The category ambition is right. MySet should not fight for "song request app." It should own **interactive live music** and the intellectual territory of **how performers, songs and crowds influence one another**.

Strategy 2.0's line survives intact:

> Song voting is the mechanism. Audience participation is the category.

### 3.2 The revised positioning stack

| Layer | MySet owns |
|---|---|
| Category | Interactive live music |
| Intellectual territory | The feedback loop between stage and room |
| Media identity | The live-music Crowd Lab |
| Audience promise | Your vote matters |
| Artist promise (emotional) | Give the room a voice without giving up the stage |
| **Artist promise (commercial)** | **A room that stays, tips, and gets you rebooked** |
| Mechanism | The audience votes on what comes next |
| Venue promise (later) | Fuller rooms, longer stays, higher bar spend |
| Long-term authority position | The company that understands what live crowds actually want |

The bolded row is new and it is the most commercially important line in this document.

### 3.3 Why the emotional promise alone will not convert

"Turn the room into part of the show" describes an experience. Working musicians are not primarily buying experiences. They are running a small business with brutal unit economics: a fee per gig, a finite number of venues in reach, and constant competition from the next guitarist with a loop pedal.

Their real questions are:

- Will this venue book me again?
- Can I raise my rate?
- Can I make more on the night than my fee?
- Will this make me look better or worse in front of the manager?

That last one is why the "nothing may break the gig" engineering principle is a marketing asset. It directly addresses the fear that adopting MySet makes the artist look like an amateur.

### 3.4 The money story (build all Convert content on this)

MySet pays for itself through four mechanisms, in ascending order of persuasive power:

1. **Tips.** Optional, immediate, small.
2. **Paid vote packs.** Novel, and the only revenue line that is genuinely new money created by the product.
3. **Longer dwell time.** An engaged room stays. A room that stays drinks. Bar spend is the venue's only metric.
4. **Rebooking leverage.** After the gig, the artist has a Room Report: how many people participated, what they wanted, what the room rated the night. No other performer walks into a venue manager's office with engagement data. This is the artist's negotiating asset and it is the strongest reason a working musician pays ten dollars a month.

Reframed as a single sentence for Convert content:

> Every other act leaves with a fee. You leave with a fee, tips, and proof the room was yours.

### 3.5 Consumer-facing language

Keep it simple and un-technical externally. Internal framing can stay as "the audience-feedback layer for live music," which usefully encompasses voting, requests, ratings, tips, and eventual venue intelligence.

**Approved external lines:**

- Live music, chosen together. *(fan-facing, brand)*
- Turn the room into part of the show. *(brand, hero)*
- Stop guessing. Let the room tell you what it wants next. *(artist-facing, top of funnel)*
- The room votes. The venue rebooks. *(artist-facing, conversion)*
- No app. No account. They just scan and vote. *(objection killer, use constantly)*

### 3.6 Positioning against alternatives

MySet's real competitive set is not other apps. It is the status quo behaviours that already solve the problem badly:

| Competitive alternative | Its weakness | MySet's counter |
|---|---|---|
| Shouted requests | Loudest voice wins, biased sample, unmemorable, interrupts the set | The quiet majority gets a vote |
| A paper request slip / notebook | Illegible, slow, no data, looks amateur | Scan, vote, ranked instantly |
| Just playing the planned set | No responsiveness, dead rooms stay dead | Real-time information between songs |
| Venue jukebox or background playlist systems | Replaces the performer rather than serving them | Performer keeps control and the stage |
| Doing nothing | Free | Free for four gigs a month |

The last row matters. Your primary competitor is inertia, and inertia is free. That is why §5's offer exists.

## 4. The buyer

### 4.1 Primary ICP (confirmed and tightened)

**Working solo artists, duos and small cover acts playing roughly five or more audience-facing gigs per month** in bars, restaurants, hotels, resorts, beach clubs and function venues.

Qualifying signals, in priority order:

1. Repertoire of roughly 80+ songs. The question "what next?" must be real, not theoretical.
2. Sets of two hours or longer. Long sets create genuine sequencing problems.
3. Audience is present for the venue, not specifically for the artist. This is the crucial distinguisher. A crowd that came to see *you* wants *your* set. A crowd that came to drink wants *their* songs.
4. Already receives shouted requests and finds them annoying.
5. Paid per gig by a venue, and cares about rebooking.
6. Performs solo or as a duo, so has hands free to touch a phone between songs.

### 4.2 Anti-ICP (do not build content for these)

- Bedroom producers and beat makers
- Original bands playing short showcase sets
- Touring artists with fixed production
- Orchestras, choirs, classical
- Wedding bands with contractually locked setlists
- DJs *(adjacent and interesting, genuinely different behaviour, defer)*
- Music fans as buyers *(they are reach, never revenue)*

### 4.3 Where they actually are

This is the section Strategy 2.0 omits entirely, and it is more actionable than any calendar.

| Location | Density | Access method | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instagram, posting gig clips with venue tags | Very high | DM, comment, collaboration | **1** |
| Facebook Groups for gigging and function musicians | Very high, underused by competitors | Value-first participation, never link-drop | **1** |
| Local venue circuits, physically | High | Walk in, watch the set, talk between sets | **1 (Scene Zero)** |
| TikTok, gigging musician creators | Medium-high, younger skew | Duet, stitch, comment | 2 |
| YouTube, searching for setlist and cover song advice | High intent, low competition | Search-optimised content | 2 |
| Reddit musician and gigging subreddits | Medium, hostile to promotion | Genuine participation only | 3 |
| Agency and booking rosters | Medium, gatekept | Partnership motion, later | 3 |
| Venue managers | Low for now | Via artists, later | 4 |

**Implication:** the fastest path to the first hundred activated gigs is a named list of individually identifiable humans, not a broadcast. Organic content warms the list. It does not replace working it.

### 4.4 Willingness to pay

Be honest internally about this. Musicians are low-propensity software buyers. The ten dollar Plus tier is cheap in absolute terms and expensive in psychological terms, because it competes against a free tier that covers four gigs and against the deeply held belief that gear is worth paying for and software is not.

Three consequences:

1. **Do not sell the subscription in content.** Sell the gig. The free tier does the selling. A five-plus gig per month artist crosses into paid by natural usage, which is exactly how the pricing was designed.
2. **Frame Plus as a business tool, not a feature unlock.** "Unlimited shows" is a feature. "The month you go from four gigs to six is the month you went pro" is a story.
3. **Build the ROI proof into the product output.** If the Room Report shows twenty-two dollars in tips and vote packs from one night, the ten dollars argues itself.

### 4.5 The avatar in one paragraph

Write this on the wall. Every piece of content is for him or her.

> A guitarist-singer in their late twenties to forties with about a hundred and fifty songs in their head and a hundred more they half-know. They play three-hour slots in bars and resorts four to eight times a month for a fee that has not risen in two years. They are good. They can read a room by instinct. But somewhere around the second hour on a Wednesday, they genuinely do not know whether this room wants Valerie or Wonderwall or to be left alone, and the wrong choice costs them fifteen minutes of energy they will not get back. They are not looking for software. They are looking for the gig to go well and the manager to text them next month.

## 5. The offer architecture

Strategy 2.0 has no offer. This section is entirely new and is, alongside §6 and §9, the highest-leverage addition in v3.0.

### 5.1 The value equation applied

Perceived value rises with dream outcome and perceived likelihood of achieving it, and falls with time delay and effort required. Right now MySet scores as follows:

| Variable | Current state | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Dream outcome | Decent. A better gig. | Raise it: a rebooked gig, a higher rate, money on the night. |
| Perceived likelihood | **Poor.** One gig of evidence, no testimonials, unknown brand. | Founding artist cohort, filmed proof, public data. |
| Time delay | Poor. Value only appears at the next gig, days away. | Same-week activation push, gig-day reminders. |
| Effort and sacrifice | **Very poor.** Import a huge repertoire, learn a Studio, risk looking silly on stage. | Concierge import, dry-run mode, one-page stage card. |

The two catastrophic scores are perceived likelihood and effort. Both are solvable without writing much code.

### 5.2 The Founding 50 Artists programme

The core acquisition offer for the next ninety days.

**What the artist gets:**

- Twelve months of Plus, free
- Their entire repertoire imported for them, by you, from a spreadsheet, a photo of a setlist, or a Spotify playlist link
- A fifteen minute setup call and a dry run before their first real show
- A printed, gig-ready QR table card and stage card mailed or sent as a print-ready PDF
- Their first gig's Room Report designed as shareable content, made for them
- Founding artist badge on their MySet artist page
- Direct line to the founder

**What you get:**

- Permission to use their gig footage, data and name in MySet content
- A short recorded reaction after the first gig
- One written or filmed testimonial after gig three
- Honest product feedback

**Why this works:** it removes the two catastrophic variables at once. Effort goes to near zero because you do the import. Perceived likelihood rises because a human is holding their hand and the downside risk of looking foolish in front of a venue manager is explicitly managed by the dry run.

**Cost to you:** time, which is the only currency you have and the correct one to spend at this stage. Fifty artists at ninety minutes each is seventy-five hours across a quarter. That is the highest-return seventy-five hours available to this company.

### 5.3 Friction demolition checklist

Every one of these is worth more than a viral Reel:

- [ ] Bulk import from a pasted list, one song per line, no formatting rules
- [ ] Import from a Spotify or YouTube playlist URL
- [ ] A starter setlist library by venue type: Irish pub, beach bar, resort dinner, sports bar, function
- [ ] Dry-run or practice mode so an artist can rehearse the flow at home
- [ ] Printable QR table tent and stage card, generated automatically, brand-clean
- [ ] A one-screen "your first gig" checklist inside the Studio
- [ ] Offline or bad-Wi-Fi graceful degradation, and a visible promise about it
- [ ] Gig-day reminder to the artist by email or SMS

### 5.4 The activation ladder and the offer at each rung

| Rung | Artist state | Offer / CTA | Channel |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Saw a video | Follow, or watch the next one | Social |
| 1 | Curious | "Send me your setlist and I'll set it up for you" | Comment, DM, link in bio |
| 2 | Signed up | Concierge import, free | Email, DM |
| 3 | Songs loaded | Dry run, QR card sent | Call |
| 4 | First gig run | Room Report made into content for them | Automated + manual |
| 5 | Second gig | Founding artist badge, feature in content | Product + social |
| 6 | Five gigs a month | Plus, naturally | Product |

Note that rungs 1 through 3 are entirely human and entirely unscalable. That is correct for the first fifty. Automate only after the manual version is proven.

### 5.5 The venue offer (hold until Scene One)

Do not build venue content yet. But hold this in reserve, because it is the endgame:

> Give us one Friday. We will put five MySet artists in five venues in one weekend and publish everything. Your venue gets the footage, the crowd data, and the busiest room on the strip.

That is the lightning strike described in §10.4.

## 6. Channel strategy and distribution physics

The most important missing section from Strategy 2.0. A content plan without a channel plan cannot be executed.

### 6.1 Channel roles

| Channel | Role | Priority | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Instagram Reels** | Primary reach and primary ICP habitat | **1** | Gigging musicians live here and post their own gig clips. Highest density of identifiable ICP. Strong DM culture makes outbound possible on the same platform as reach. |
| **TikTok** | Secondary reach, format R&D | 2 | Best pure discovery engine, best place to test participation formats, weaker ICP density and weaker DM culture. |
| **YouTube Shorts** | Distribution overflow | 3 | Cross-post the same vertical asset. Low marginal cost, non-trivial upside. |
| **YouTube long-form / search** | Compounding authority and high-intent capture | **2, rising to 1 over twelve months** | "Best songs for a bar gig" and similar queries are searched constantly by the exact ICP, are permanently indexed, and are barely contested by any competitor. Missing entirely from Strategy 2.0. |
| **Facebook Groups** | Direct ICP access | **1** | Gigging and function musician groups are enormous, extremely active, and mostly ignored by tech marketers. Rules are strict. Participate genuinely; never link-drop. |
| **Email** | Owned audience, the only durable asset | **1** | Everything else is rented. |
| **Offline / venue circuit** | Scene Zero engine | **1 in Koh Phangan** | You are physically in a dense live-music market. Walking into venues is faster than any funnel. |
| Reddit | Listening and occasional depth | 4 | Useful for research and language mining. Hostile to promotion. |
| X / Threads | Build-in-public, founder network | 4 | Low ICP density, some investor and operator value. |
| LinkedIn | Venue, hospitality and partnership motion | 5 | Later, for the venue and B2B layer. |

### 6.2 Instagram doctrine

- **Format:** 9:16, seven to thirty seconds for Attract, thirty to sixty for Position, sixty to ninety for Nurture.
- **Hook window:** first 1.5 seconds must contain the choice, the stake, or the tension. Text on screen plus spoken hook, not one or the other.
- **Sound:** real gig audio wherever possible. Crowd noise is a credibility signal. Trending audio only for Chaos Agent tests.
- **Captions:** always burned in, high contrast, positioned above the UI safe zone.
- **Carousels:** underrated for Position content. A seven-slide "how to sequence a three-hour set" saves well and saves are the strongest ranking signal for authority content.
- **Stories:** the daily heartbeat. Behind the scenes, polls, gig-day content, artist reposts. Cheap, no algorithm risk, and where your warmest people are.
- **Collab posts:** post as a collaboration with the featured artist wherever possible. This is the single highest-leverage growth mechanic available to you, and it costs nothing. Every Founding 50 artist should be a collab post.

### 6.3 TikTok doctrine

- Faster hooks, more chaotic, higher tolerance for raw footage.
- **Stitch and duet bait is the core mechanic.** Post a setlist dilemma and explicitly invite stitches. Ask musicians to duet with what they would play.
- Series feature for franchises.
- Reply-with-video to comments is the strongest and most underused loop for participation content. See §7.6.

### 6.4 YouTube search doctrine (new, and strategically important)

This is where the compounding happens. Short video is a slot machine. Search is an annuity.

**Target query clusters:**

| Cluster | Example queries | Content type |
|---|---|---|
| Repertoire | best cover songs for bar gigs, songs that always work live, crowd pleasers for pubs | List, updated annually |
| Set construction | how to build a three hour setlist, what to open with, what to close with | Framework video |
| Crowd management | how to handle song requests, what to do when the crowd is dead, drunk requests | Story + framework |
| Business | how much to charge for a bar gig, how to get rebooked, how to pitch venues | High commercial intent |
| Gear and logistics | solo acoustic gig setup, backing tracks for solo gigs | Adjacent, high volume |

The Business cluster is the highest value and the least served. "How to get rebooked at a bar" is a query written by someone with a wallet and a problem, and MySet's answer to it is genuinely differentiated because of the Room Report.

**Cadence:** one long-form video per week is unrealistic at first. Start at one per fortnight, ten to eighteen minutes, and let every long-form video generate four to six Shorts.

**Note:** I cannot verify current search volumes for these queries. Validate them with a keyword tool before committing production time.

### 6.5 Email and owned audience

Non-negotiable and completely absent from Strategy 2.0.

**Lead magnets, in order of expected performance:**

1. **The Crowd-Pleaser 100.** A curated, opinionated list of songs that reliably work live, segmented by venue type and decade. High perceived value, trivially shareable, directly on-ramps to "now import these into MySet in one click."
2. **The Dead Room Playbook.** A short PDF on what to do in the eleven most common gig failure states.
3. **The Venue Pitch Kit.** An email template plus a one-page leave-behind for pitching venues, with a slot for engagement data. Ties directly to the rebooking promise.

**Sequence after opt-in:** five emails over ten days moving from useful, to useful, to story, to soft offer, to Founding 50 invitation.

**Why this matters most:** the Crowd-Pleaser 100 and the Venue Pitch Kit are the same asset class as MySet's eventual Live Music Index. Building the list and building the moat are the same activity.

### 6.6 Account architecture

Make this decision now, because it is expensive to reverse.

**Recommended: dual-account, founder-led.**

| Account | Handle concept | Content | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Founder / musician account** | A new music-specific identity, separate from @perry.idyll | Nurture, Journey, Expert, on-camera, opinionated | The trust engine. People follow people. |
| **MySet brand account** | @myset (or as available) | Attract franchises, Crowd Lab data, artist features, Convert | The category engine. Scales beyond you. |

**Why separate from @perry.idyll:** that account is a wellness and personal growth identity. Mixing a gigging-musician software audience into it damages both. The algorithm will also struggle to categorise a hybrid account, which suppresses distribution for both topics.

**Cross-posting rule:** brand account posts everything. Founder account posts the human half, and appears in brand content as the face. Founder account grows faster early; brand account compounds later.

**If capacity forces one account:** choose the brand account with your face in it constantly. Never a faceless brand account.

### 6.7 Borrowed audience, the cold-start solvent

The plan needs distribution before it has an audience. Three mechanisms, in order of leverage:

1. **Collab posts with Founding 50 artists.** Every artist has a following of hundreds to thousands of exactly the right people: their fans, and their musician peers. Fifty collab posts is fifty borrowed audiences.
2. **Creator collaborations.** Identify ten to fifteen gigging-musician creators with between five thousand and a hundred thousand followers. Do not pitch a sponsorship. Pitch a format: "let your audience pick your next set, on camera, and we will build it." That is a good video for them for free, and a perfect demo for you.
3. **Comment presence.** Thirty minutes daily leaving genuinely good comments on gigging-musician content. Not "great post." Actual insight. This is slow, unglamorous, and reliably works for a new account.

## 7. The content operating system

### 7.1 The two axes, retained

Strategy 2.0's structural insight is correct and becomes the spine of the automation.

- **Axis A, topic territory:** three buckets plus a Chaos slot.
- **Axis B, funnel job:** Attract, Nurture, Position (Expert), Position (Journey), Convert.

Every post is a cell in that matrix. Every automated brief specifies both coordinates. Neither axis alone is a sufficient brief.

### 7.2 The buckets, refined

**Bucket 1 — YOU PICK THE SET.** Broad-but-adjacent reach. The internet participates. Every hook contains a live-performance frame. The psychological objective is to condition the participation behaviour before anyone touches the product.

**Bucket 2 — READ THE ROOM.** Musician authority. Frameworks, not tips. The test for every piece: would a musician who will never sign up still find this valuable? If no, rewrite it.

**Bucket 3 — CROWD LAB.** Proof and category creation. Real gigs, predictions, results, product experiments, honest failures.

**Slot 4 — CHAOS AGENT.** One weekly format experiment with no obligation to fit the system. This is the R&D budget. Protect it.

**Pillar 5 — THE LIBRARY (new).** Not a social bucket. The search-and-owned-audience layer from §6.4 and §6.5. Long-form, evergreen, indexed. Runs on a fortnightly cadence, independent of the daily rhythm.

### 7.3 Dynamic funnel weighting (correction to Strategy 2.0)

Jun Yuh's 3/1/2/1 is a mature-creator ratio. Applying it at stage zero misallocates effort into Convert content that has nothing to convert with and Attract content the account cannot yet distribute.

| Stage | Trigger | A | N | P-Expert | P-Journey | C | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Ignition** | 0–10 real gigs | 3 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 0 hard, Convert embedded softly | Journey is the only honest content available and is what recruits early adopters. Hard Convert with no proof burns credibility. |
| **B — Proof** | 10–50 gigs | 3 | 1 | 2 | 1 | 1 | The Jun Yuh baseline. Proof now exists to convert with. |
| **C — Scale** | 50+ gigs, data emerging | 3 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 | Proof is abundant. Push conversion and let the data do the positioning. |

Transitions are triggered by evidence, not by dates.

### 7.4 The format-level qualification rule (new)

Strategy 2.0 fixes audience quality at the level of the topic. Fix it at the level of the format too.

A text-on-screen poll asking "which song saves a dead bar?" attracts fans.
A visibly working musician, on a real stage, in bad light, holding a guitar, asking the same question attracts musicians and fans, and converts the musicians.

**Rule: the frame of every Attract post should contain at least one live-performance signal.** A stage, an instrument, a venue, gig audio, a performer's POV, or the MySet tally. Never a bare graphic.

This costs nothing and roughly doubles the ICP density of the reach you buy.

### 7.5 Hook architecture

Every hook compresses **context + stakes + choice** into the first second.

| Hook family | Template | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Constraint | "You have [limit]. [Choice]." | "One song left. Packed bar. Go." |
| Rescue | "[Failure state]. Which one saves it?" | "Room's dead. Which of these brings it back?" |
| Betrayal | "They voted for [X]. Are you playing it?" | "The crowd voted for Wonderwall. Fourth time this week." |
| Elimination | "One [thing] disappears forever." | "Delete one cover song from every bar on earth." |
| Prediction | "I predicted [X]. The room chose [Y]." | "I was wrong about all five." |
| Contradiction | "[Common belief] is wrong." | "The loudest request is never the room's request." |
| Confession | "[Embarrassing founder truth]." | "I locked myself out of the app I built. Mid-gig." |
| Insider | "[Thing only performers know]." | "Every gigging musician knows the 9:40 dip." |
| Data | "[Number] [surprising unit]." | "Twenty-one votes. Eight people. One purchase. Here's what shocked me." |

**Banned:** "Favourite song?", "Comment below!", "Let me know what you think." Low-stakes prompts produce low-value engagement and teach the algorithm that your audience is passive.

### 7.6 The comment section is the product

For a participation product, the comment section is simultaneously the demo, the research, and the distribution engine. Strategy 2.0 ignores it entirely.

**Mechanics to run on every Attract post:**

1. **Pin the tally.** Within two hours, pin a comment showing the live count. "Currently: Valerie 41, Wonderwall 38, Tennessee Whiskey 22." The comment section literally becomes a MySet screen.
2. **Reply with video** to the three best or most wrong comments. This creates free follow-up content and the platform rewards it heavily.
3. **The two-post loop.** Post one gathers the public vote. Post two reveals the result, or compares the internet's answer against what a real room chose. One piece manufactures the next, forever.
4. **Wrong-answer bait.** Include one deliberately contentious option. Disagreement outperforms agreement.
5. **Mine every comment.** Comments are the language bank, the objection list, and the research pipeline. §9 automates the harvest.

### 7.7 The franchise portfolio

Recurring formats beat isolated posts. Strategy 2.0's list is good. Ranked here by expected leverage, with launch order.

| # | Franchise | Job | Launch | Why this order |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **SAVE THE GIG** | Attract | Week 1 | Highest stakes, clearest game, most transposable. |
| 2 | **READ THE ROOM** | Position, Expert | Week 1 | The authority spine. Works with zero users. |
| 3 | **BUILDING MYSET** | Nurture / Journey | Week 1 | The only fully honest content at stage zero, and it recruits. |
| 4 | **ARTIST vs AUDIENCE** | Position, Journey | Week 3, needs one artist | The flagship. Prediction versus reality. |
| 5 | **ONE HAS TO GO** | Attract | Week 2 | Elimination is reliably viral and endlessly repeatable. |
| 6 | **ROOM REPORT** | Position / Convert | Week 4, needs gigs | The proof machine and the future product feature. |
| 7 | **TRY THIS TONIGHT** | Convert | Week 5, Stage B | Concrete demonstration. Hold until proof exists. |
| 8 | **REQUEST DENIED** | Nurture / Attract | Week 4 | Performer war stories. Great for creator collabs. |
| 9 | **THE PERFECT SET** | Attract | Week 6 | Constraint game, high save rate. |
| 10 | **WHAT WON?** | Attract / Position | Week 6 | Reveal mechanic, pairs with the two-post loop. |
| 11 | **CROWD CONTROL** | Flagship event | Scene One | The lightning strike. Needs density. |

**Naming discipline:** a franchise needs a fixed name, a fixed opening frame, a fixed visual treatment, and a fixed length. Recognition is the entire point. Eventually people should recognise ARTIST vs AUDIENCE before the logo.

### 7.8 Objection-to-story matrix

Strategy 2.0's table is good. Extended here, with the critical rule restated: **never turn these into feature walkthroughs. Turn each into a situation.**

| Objection | Situation to dramatise | Mechanism revealed second |
|---|---|---|
| "My crowd won't download anything." | Film a stranger scanning and voting in under eight seconds | QR to browser, no account |
| "I don't want the crowd running my gig." | You reject a voted song on camera and explain why | Studio control, you choose the pool |
| "I'll get five hundred stupid requests." | A ridiculous request arriving and being declined in one tap | Requests toggle, accept/decline |
| "They'll request what I just played." | Someone requests the song from twenty minutes ago | Replay cost |
| "My repertoire is 200 songs." | Time-lapse of a bulk import | Paste list, import |
| "How do I know what they wanted?" | Reading the Room Report the next morning, coffee, hungover | Past show data, unmet demand |
| "Will this make me money?" | Tips notification landing mid-set | Tips, vote packs |
| "What if the Wi-Fi dies?" | The literal worst-case, handled | Nothing may break the gig |
| "I'll look like an amateur." | Venue manager reacting to a full, loud room | Dwell time, rebooking |
| "I already read rooms fine." | You predict five songs and get three wrong | Prediction versus result |

The last one is the most important and the most under-used. It is the only piece of content that makes an experienced, confident performer feel a gap. Confidence is the real objection.

## 8. The gig-to-content industrial pipeline

One gig must not produce one Reel. One gig should produce a fortnight of material across every funnel job.

### 8.1 Pre-production: design the payoff before you shoot

The most common failure is shooting a gig and then hunting for a story in the footage. Invert it.

**Before every filmed gig, write down:**

1. The one question this gig answers. *("Will the crowd pick the songs I expect?")*
2. The prediction, recorded on camera, before doors.
3. The three hooks you intend to cut. Write the titles first.
4. The one moment that, if it happens, is the whole video. Assign someone to catch it.

If you cannot name the question and the prediction beforehand, you are documenting, not experimenting. Documenting produces one mediocre Reel. Experimenting produces a franchise.

### 8.2 The shot list

Expanded from Strategy 2.0. Print this. Give it to whoever is holding the second phone.

| # | Moment | Shot | Becomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Pre-gig, empty room | Artist to camera, prediction of top 3–5 | Journey opener, ARTIST vs AUDIENCE part 1 |
| 2 | Pre-gig | Setlist build and QR placement, sped up | Convert, TRY THIS TONIGHT |
| 3 | Empty venue | Wide establishing, ambient | Narrative context, B-roll |
| 4 | Doors, crowd arriving | Wide, real audio | Social proof |
| 5 | First scan | Close on a stranger's hands and phone | The single most important Convert shot |
| 6 | Votes arriving | Screen capture of Studio, unbroken | Tension, scoreboard motif |
| 7 | Lead change | Screen capture at the moment top 1 changes | WHAT WON, high-drama cut |
| 8 | Artist sees the winner | Face, reaction, unposed | Attract, the emotional payoff |
| 9 | The song being played | 15–20s of performance, real audio, crowd singing | Payoff, proof |
| 10 | An unexpected request | Screenshot plus artist reaction | REQUEST DENIED |
| 11 | A failure | Anything that breaks or confuses someone | Nurture, and the most trustworthy content you own |
| 12 | Crowd singing | Wide, phone held high | The shot that sells the whole product |
| 13 | End of night | Final vote data on screen | ROOM REPORT |
| 14 | Immediately after | 60s unedited artist reaction, sweaty, honest | Position and Nurture |
| 15 | Fan vox pop | 10s, "did you vote? what did you vote for?" | Convert, testimonial |
| 16 | Venue manager | Optional, 30s reaction | Future venue content, rebooking proof |

Sixteen moments. Realistically twelve get captured. Twelve is enough for eight to fourteen assets.

### 8.3 The multiplier

One well-covered gig yields:

| Asset | Count | Funnel job |
|---|---|---|
| Prediction video | 1 | Position, Journey |
| Reveal video | 1 | Position, Journey |
| Crowd singalong clip | 1–2 | Attract |
| Product demo, scan to vote | 1 | Convert |
| Room Report card and voiceover | 1 | Position |
| Artist reaction | 1 | Nurture |
| Fan vox pop cutdown | 1 | Convert |
| Failure or funny moment | 1 | Nurture |
| Stories set | 8–15 frames | Warm audience |
| Carousel, what we learned | 1 | Position |
| YouTube long-form, the full experiment | 0.5 | Search, authority |

**Target: 10 to 14 publishable assets per filmed gig.** At two filmed gigs a month you have covered roughly half of a seven-a-week cadence with proof content, and the remaining half is studio-shootable Attract and Expert material that can be batched.

### 8.4 Batch production model (the capacity fix)

Seven posts a week, shot daily, by a solo founder building a product, will fail. Structure it as three sessions.

| Session | When | Duration | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Studio batch** | Every second Monday | 3 hours | 8–10 Attract and Expert pieces, shot back to back, one setup, outfit changes optional and honestly unnecessary |
| **Gig capture** | 1–2 Fridays or Saturdays a month | The gig | 10–14 assets |
| **Edit and schedule block** | Every Sunday | 2–3 hours | Week ahead scheduled, comments queued, hooks logged |

Total: roughly eight to eleven hours a fortnight for a seven-a-week cadence. That is achievable. Daily improvisation is not.

**Minimum viable cadence if a week collapses:** three posts. One Attract, one Position, one Story-only day. Never zero. Never a burst of seven to catch up.

Required for the automation in §9 to function. Every file, every row in the database:

```
2026-09-12_CROWDLAB_JOURNEY_ARTISTVSAUDIENCE_first-prediction-miss_v2
```

| Field | Values |
|---|---|
| BUCKET | YPTS, RTR, CROWDLAB, CHAOS, LIBRARY |
| JOB | ATTRACT, NURTURE, EXPERT, JOURNEY, CONVERT |
| FRANCHISE | Fixed franchise code or NONE |
| SLUG | Human-readable, kebab-case |
| VERSION | v1, v2, platform variants as v2-tt, v2-ig |

## 9. The automation engine

This is the section that turns the strategy into a machine. It is designed as an extension of the multi-agent content intelligence architecture you already built, rather than a new system from scratch.

### 9.1 Architecture overview

```
  [1] INGEST  ──▶  [2] ANALYSE  ──▶  [3] GENERATE  ──▶  [4] HUMAN GATE
     │                  │                  │                   │
     │                  │                  │                   ▼
     │                  │                  │            [5] PRODUCE
     │                  │                  │                   │
     ▼                  ▼                  ▼                   ▼
  Sources DB      Pattern DB         Brief DB           [6] PUBLISH
     ▲                                                         │
     │                                                         ▼
     └──────────────  [8] LEARN  ◀────────────  [7] MEASURE ────┘
```

Eight stages. Stages 1, 2, 3, 7 and 8 automate well. Stage 4 must never be removed. Stages 5 and 6 partially automate.

### 9.2 Stage by stage

**[1] INGEST — automated, daily**

Collect into a structured store:
- Outlier posts from the 25-account watchlist (§9.5), with transcript, hook, format, metrics
- Comments on your own posts, raw
- Facebook Group and Reddit threads matching gigging-musician keywords
- MySet product events: shows started, votes cast, unmet requests, tips
- Search query data once YouTube and site content exist

**[2] ANALYSE — automated, weekly**

Run the creator-analyst agent panel. Your existing roster of six is a strong base. For MySet, revise it:

| Agent | Lens | Keep / Add |
|---|---|---|
| Jun Yuh | Funnel job allocation, post-level intent | Keep |
| Alex Hormozi | Offer strength, value equation, CTA quality | Keep |
| Gary Vaynerchuk | Atomisation, pillar-to-fragment, volume discipline | Keep |
| Donald Miller | Narrative clarity, is the musician the hero | Keep |
| Kallaway | Hook engineering, first-second retention | Keep |
| Daniel Priestley | Authority assets, oversubscription, ascension | Keep |
| **MrBeast-style retention analyst** | Payoff placement, does the promise land, re-watch triggers | **Add** |
| **April Dunford positioning analyst** | Competitive alternatives, unique attributes, is the claim differentiated | **Add** |
| **Category design analyst (Play Bigger)** | Is this building a category or a product, lightning-strike readiness | **Add** |
| **Search demand analyst** | Query intent, evergreen potential, indexability | **Add** |
| **Live-music domain analyst** | Does a working musician find this true, is the language insider-correct | **Add** |

Synthesis layer produces: this week's three highest-confidence format bets, the two formats to kill, and the hook patterns that produced outliers.

**[3] GENERATE — automated, weekly**

Produces the week's seven briefs. **A brief, not a script.** Each brief contains:

```yaml
date: 2026-09-14
bucket: YPTS
job: ATTRACT
franchise: SAVE_THE_GIG
hook: "Room's dead. Fifteen minutes to closing. Which one?"
stakes: explicit failure state, closing time pressure
choice_set: [4 songs, one deliberately contentious]
live_signal: shot on stage, guitar in frame, gig audio
payoff: reveal at 6s, "and here's what actually happened"
comment_mechanic: pin tally at +2h, reply-video to top wrong answer
cta: soft, "send me your setlist and I'll set it up"
loop: pairs with WHAT_WON reveal on 2026-09-17
platform_variants: [IG 9:16 12s, TT 9:16 15s w/ stitch bait, Shorts cross]
evidence_check: PASS — no claims made
```

**[4] HUMAN GATE — never automate**

You approve or reject every brief. Two checks:
1. **The truth check.** Does a working musician read this and think "yes, exactly"? Only you can answer that.
2. **The evidence check.** §13. Does any claim exceed what actually happened?

Budget: fifteen minutes a week. This gate is what keeps the engine from producing confident nonsense.

**[5] PRODUCE — partially automated**

- Shot lists, captions, on-screen text, and thumbnail concepts: generated
- Filming and performance: human, always
- Editing: human or editor, with generated cut sheets
- Room Report cards: fully automatable from product data, and should be a product feature

**[6] PUBLISH — automated scheduling, human posting for high-stakes**

Schedule everything. Post Chaos Agent and collab posts manually so you can be present in comments for the first hour, which materially affects distribution.

**[7] MEASURE — automated, weekly**

Pull metrics per §12 into the same store, joined on the asset ID from §8.5. Segment by funnel job. Never compare a Convert post to an Attract post.

**[8] LEARN — automated, weekly, feeding back to [1]**

Update the pattern database with what worked. Promote winning hooks into the hook bank. Demote formats hitting kill criteria.

### 9.3 The content database schema

Minimum viable tables. Any store works, a spreadsheet is fine to start.

**`assets`**
`id, date, bucket, job, franchise, hook_text, hook_family, format, length_s, live_signal (bool), platform, url, status`

**`performance`**
`asset_id, platform, views, non_follower_pct, hold_3s, completion, saves, shares, comments, profile_visits, link_clicks, collected_at`

**`hooks`**
`id, text, family, source (own/watchlist), best_performing_asset_id, avg_hold_3s, times_used, verdict`

**`artists`**
`id, name, handle, city, gigs_per_month, stage (0-6 per §5.4), founding_50 (bool), first_gig_date, gigs_run, footage_rights (bool), last_contact`

**`gigs`**
`id, artist_id, venue, date, voters, votes, requests, unmet_requests, tips, vote_pack_revenue, rating, assets_generated, prediction_accuracy`

**`objections`**
`id, text, source, frequency, mapped_content_id, status`

The `artists` and `gigs` tables matter more than the content tables. They are the actual business, and they are also the content supply chain.

### 9.4 The hook bank

Every outlier hook, from your content and the watchlist, gets logged with its family (§7.5), its structure abstracted, and its performance. Over ninety days this becomes the most valuable file in the company: a proven, MySet-specific library the generation agent draws from instead of inventing.

**Rule:** the generator may only use hook families with logged evidence, or explicitly flag a new family as an experiment routed to the Chaos slot.

> 2. Hook structure and hook family

> 6. Story structure
> 7. Target audience, and specifically whether it reaches performers or only fans
> 8. Relevance to working live musicians

> H. **Any format whose success depends on scale, audience size or data we do not yet have — flag these as deferred**

### 9.7 What must never be automated

- The truth check. Whether a line is true to a working musician.
- The evidence check. §13.
- On-camera performance and reaction.
- Replies to real artists in DMs and comments. The Founding 50 relationship is the asset.
- Any claim about data, results or user numbers.

## 10. The cold start: first ninety days

### 10.1 The sequencing correction

Strategy 2.0 launches a content calendar. That is the wrong first move because the calendar depends on gigs that do not exist.

**Correct order:**

```
Artists ──▶ Gigs ──▶ Footage + Data ──▶ Content ──▶ More Artists
```

The loop is self-reinforcing once started, but it must be started manually at the artist end. Weeks one to four are an outbound recruitment sprint with a content layer, not a content launch with an outbound layer.

### 10.2 The outbound motion

**Target: 50 conversations, 20 signups, 12 first gigs, 7 repeat gigs, in 90 days.**

**Lead sourcing, all free and all public:**
- Instagram location tags for bars and venues in target scenes
- Hashtags: gigging musician, solo acoustic, cover artist, pub gig, function band, plus local variants
- Venue Instagram accounts, then the artists they tag
- Facebook Groups for gigging and function musicians
- Local listings, "live music tonight" pages
- Physical: walk into venues, watch a set, talk between sets

**The DM, written to be answered:**

> Hey [name], saw your set at [venue] on the [date] post. The [specific song] clip. I'm a musician building a thing where the room scans a QR and votes on what you play next, no app, no signup for them. I'm setting up 50 artists personally, free, and I'll import your whole songlist myself so you don't have to do anything. Want me to set yours up for this weekend?

Specific, not templated. References an actual post. Leads with the offer, not the product. Removes the effort explicitly.

**Cadence:** ten personalised DMs a day, five days a week. That is fifty a week, roughly six hundred over the quarter. A five to ten percent conversation rate gets you to the target.

### 10.3 Scene Zero: Koh Phangan

You are physically in a live-music market with unusual properties: high venue density, a large population of working cover performers, an English-speaking transient audience with high turnover, low gatekeeping, and short distances between venues.

That makes it close to ideal as a **proof and footage laboratory**, and imperfect as a **market signal**, because the audience is transient and the venue economics differ from Western markets.

Use it accordingly:

| Use Koh Phangan for | Do not use it for |
|---|---|
| Filming real gigs, quickly and repeatedly | Concluding that the product works everywhere |
| Testing the artist onboarding flow in person | Pricing validation |
| Building the first ten to fifteen Founding artists | Venue economics conclusions |
| Producing the entire visual asset library | The Live Music Index dataset |

**Target: ten Koh Phangan artists running real gigs within sixty days.** That is achievable through physical presence alone and gives you the footage library that makes everything else possible.

### 10.4 Scene One: the first exported scene

Choose one Western market by criteria, not affinity:

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Density of cover and function gigs per square mile | High |
| Strong existing online gigging-musician community | High |
| English language | High |
| Venue culture where the performer chooses the set | High |
| Your ability to reach artists remotely by DM | Medium |
| Eventual venue-side expansion potential | Medium |

Candidates worth evaluating: Nashville, Austin, Dublin, Brighton, Manchester, Gold Coast, Auckland. Pick one. Do not pick three.

**The Scene One lightning strike:** the Crowd-Controlled Weekend. Five artists, five venues, one weekend, every room voting, everything filmed. Publish for a month afterwards. This is simultaneously growth, content, PR, product research and sales, and it is the moment category creation becomes visible from outside.

Strategy 2.0 places this in Phase 3. It belongs at the end of Phase 1, roughly day seventy-five to ninety, as the culmination of the first quarter.

### 10.5 Ninety-day phase map

| Phase | Days | Primary objective | Content posture | Success gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — Preflight** | -14 to 0 | Accounts, franchises defined, watchlist built, Founding 50 page live, concierge import process written, lead list of 150 artists sourced | Nothing published | Ten assets in the bank before day one |

| Sun | Chaos | — | Give five songs to a performer, crowd picks the one they must play immediately. |

**Week 4 — First proof cycle closes.**

| Day | Job | Franchise | Post |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | Attract | YPTS | "Gen Z vs Millennials: who picks the better encore?" |
| Tue | Expert | READ THE ROOM | "When should you actually abandon the set you planned?" |
| Wed | Attract | SAVE THE GIG | "Three hours in. Which one wakes the room up?" |
| Thu | Convert | TRY THIS TONIGHT | Full setup walkthrough plus the Founding 50 offer, hard. |
| Fri | Nurture | REQUEST DENIED | The worst genuine request, and what you did. |
| Sat | Journey | ARTIST vs AUDIENCE | **The reveal. Prediction versus what the room actually chose.** |
| Sun | Chaos | — | Strangers collectively build a ten-song set in public. |

**Contingency:** if no artist has run a gig by week three, replace both Saturday slots with BUILDING MYSET and run your own gig as the subject. Never fabricate. Never postpone the whole calendar.

### 11.3 The two-post loop, formalised

Monday gathers a vote. Wednesday or Saturday reveals the result, or compares the internet's answer against a real room's. This means:

- Every Monday manufactures a later post at zero additional ideation cost
- Every reveal has a built-in reason for the original voters to return
- Public votes accumulate as genuine research

Over twelve weeks that is twelve public datasets, which is the seed of the Live Music Index long before you have enough gigs to build it from product data.

## 12. Measurement and the decision framework

### 12.1 North Star

**Activated MySet gigs per week**, where activated means a real performer ran a real show and at least one external audience member voted.

A thousand signups that never start a show are worth approximately nothing. A hundred musicians using MySet in real bars is the beginning of a company.

### 12.2 The funnel

```
Viewer → Profile visit → Site → Signup → Songs imported →
Show created → Show started → First external vote → Second gig → Fifth monthly gig → Plus
```

Instrument every arrow. The two most fragile transitions are **signup to songs imported** and **show started to first external vote**. Watch those two above all others. Concierge onboarding exists to protect the first. QR placement guidance exists to protect the second.

### 12.3 Metrics by funnel job

Never judge a Convert post against a viral Attract post. Compare like against like.

| Job | Leading content metrics | Downstream business metric | Health signal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attract | Non-follower reach, 3s hold, completion, shares, comment volume | Qualified profile visits | Comments that contain gig language, not just song names |
| Nurture | Returning viewers, substantive comments, replies, DMs | Trust, repeat exposure | Musicians replying with their own stories |
| Position, Expert | **Saves**, shares to musicians, follows, qualified DMs | Artist intent | Saves are the single best proxy for authority |
| Position, Journey | Comments asking "how do I try this", follows | Artist intent | People asking to be in the experiment |
| Convert | Profile to site CTR, signups, DM offer replies | Activation | Signup rate per thousand views |

**Reframe for Attract (per §2.3):** measure Attract on reach and engagement volume, and on whether it lifts distribution of the narrow content that follows it. Do not expect it to convert directly. Its honest job is feeding the algorithm and making the account look alive.

### 12.4 Attribution mechanics

Without these you will guess. Cheap to implement, all of them:

- A unique short link per franchise, not per post. Franchise-level attribution is enough and stays manageable.
- UTM discipline on every link: source, medium, campaign equals franchise name.
- A single mandatory "how did you find MySet?" free-text field at signup. Free text, not a dropdown. The language they use is itself research.
- A per-artist referral code for Founding 50 artists, which also makes their collab posts measurable.
- Weekly join of `assets` to `performance` to signups by date proximity. Imperfect, directionally sufficient.

### 12.5 The review ritual

**Weekly, 45 minutes:**
1. Top three and bottom three assets by hold rate, within job category
2. New hooks logged to the bank
3. Artist pipeline: conversations, signups, gigs run, stuck rungs
4. One decision: what to double, what to kill

**Monthly, 2 hours:**
1. Funnel job weighting still correct for stage?
2. Franchise portfolio review against kill rules
3. Watchlist refresh, five accounts swapped
4. Offer performance: is Founding 50 converting, and where does it stall

**Quarterly:**
1. Positioning still true?
2. Stage transition A → B → C?
3. Scene expansion decision

### 12.6 Kill rules, refined

Strategy 2.0's correction is right and worth restating precisely.

**Kill fast (after 4 posts underperforming their job category):**
- A specific hook
- A specific format
- A specific franchise
- A specific visual treatment

**Never kill on view count alone:**
- A funnel job
- A topic bucket
- The Expert layer, which will always lose on views and always win on buyer quality

**The diagnostic when READ THE ROOM underperforms:** was it too generic, was the hook weak, was it talking-head sludge, did we teach the wrong subject? Never conclude "musicians don't value expertise."

**Franchise kill criteria:** four consecutive posts below the median of their job category on the primary metric for that job, with at least two distinct hooks tested. Then kill the franchise, not the bucket.

## 13. Evidence integrity rules

Strategy 2.0's strongest ethical and strategic contribution. Formalised here as binding rules for every human and every agent in the system.

### 13.1 The current evidence base

As of this document, MySet's total real-world evidence is:

- **One real gig**
- **8 voters**
- **21 votes**
- **1 purchase, $3**
- No customer-facing failures: the one purchase was delivered and played, and the founder ran the show from his own phone (an earlier draft here claimed otherwise — corrected 2026-09-04)
- 578 passing automated assertions

The previously circulated "40 phones" result was a synthetic security test using fabricated fan IDs. It is not evidence and must never appear in marketing.

### 13.2 The rules

**Rule 1.** Aspirational template language must never resemble existing evidence. "We let 80 people control the setlist" is a format description, never a claim. If it appears in a brief, it must be flagged as a template.

**Rule 2.** Demonstrable today, or framed as exploration. If a viewer can watch the video and immediately do the thing, it can be shown as a feature. If not, it is framed as "we're exploring", "we're building", or "would you use this?"

Currently not demonstrable and therefore exploration-only: venue self-service billing, push alerts, some Pro extras, venue reviews, venue speaker-music voting, and the Room Report share card.

**Rule 3.** The authority ladder. Claims scale with evidence and never ahead of it.

| Stage | Permitted claim |
|---|---|
| Now | "We're testing what happens when crowds help shape live sets." |
| 10 real gigs | "Here's what we learned from our first ten gigs." |
| 100 gigs | "Patterns are starting to emerge." |
| 1,000 gigs | "Here's what live audiences consistently do." |
| Large scale | "MySet data suggests…" |

**Rule 4.** Every number stated publicly must be traceable to a row in the `gigs` table.

**Rule 5.** Failures are publishable and are your most credible asset. Never sand them down. "Nothing exposes software faster than a bar full of real people" is better content than any success claim you could currently make.

### 13.3 Why this is an asset, not a constraint

Being early is only embarrassing if you pretend otherwise. The honest version produces a genuinely compelling narrative that a mature competitor cannot copy:

> We're at the beginning. Come help us figure this out.

Early-adopter musicians respond to that. They do not respond to a pre-revenue company performing confidence.

## 14. The long game: the intellectual moat

### 14.1 The asymmetry

Any competent developer can clone a QR voting interface in a month. Nobody can clone ten million real live-music decisions, or the brand people associate with understanding them.

MySet's defensibility is not the software. It is proprietary live-crowd intelligence: what people actually vote for, request, replay, pay for, and want but never hear.

### 14.2 Questions only MySet will eventually be able to answer

| Question | Data source |
|---|---|
| Which songs do performers systematically underestimate? | Prediction versus actual vote |
| Most requested, least played? | Requests joined to played history |
| What makes someone pay to hear a song? | Vote pack behaviour |
| Which songs get requested again after being played? | Replay demand |
| What consistently closes a room? | Time and order joined to votes |
| How do cities differ? | Aggregate by geography |
| Beach bar versus pub versus hotel? | Venue type segmentation |
| Which songs divide a room? | Vote distribution variance |
| Which songs create consensus? | Concentrated vote share |
| What does the internet call overplayed but a real room still votes for? | Social poll versus room data |

That last one is the flagship annual story and the sharpest thing MySet will ever be able to say.

### 14.3 The publication asset

Reserve the umbrella brand now: **The MySet Live Music Index**, or **The Crowd Report**.

Eventual headlines that earn coverage and backlinks:

- "The 25 most requested live songs of 2027"
- "The songs musicians underestimate most"
- "The world's most reliable closing song"
- "What 100,000 live votes taught us about crowd psychology"
- "The gap between what the internet says is overplayed and what rooms actually vote for"

**Bridge to get there before the data exists:** the two-post loop (§11.3) generates public vote datasets from week one. Twelve weeks of public votes is a legitimate, honestly-labelled first index: "what 40,000 people on the internet say, versus what eleven real rooms did." That contrast is arguably more interesting than either dataset alone, and it is available now.

### 14.4 Product-led media loop

The Room Report share card is the single highest-leverage unbuilt feature. If every musician receives a beautiful, shareable piece of content simply by using MySet, then using MySet becomes an advertisement for MySet.

```
Artist runs gig → auto-generated Room Report →
Artist posts it → Venue reposts →
Fans see their vote reflected → Another musician asks what it is →
They try it next weekend
```

Build this before any Pro feature. It is marketing infrastructure disguised as a product feature.

The last group is the business. The rest is marketing.

## Appendix B — Franchise briefs

**SAVE THE GIG** — Attract. 8–15s. Cold open on a failure state, four options, one contentious, reveal your pick at the end. Shot on stage. Gig audio. Weekly.

**READ THE ROOM** — Position, Expert. 30–60s or carousel. One framework, never a tip list. Must contain a model: a decision tree, a two-failure-modes structure, a threshold. Ends with an application, not a CTA. Weekly.

**BUILDING MYSET** — Nurture, Journey. 45–90s. One real thing that happened while building or gigging. Failure preferred. No lesson-forcing. Weekly at Stage A, fortnightly after.

**ARTIST vs AUDIENCE** — Position, Journey. Two-part. Part one: prediction to camera, pre-gig, sealed. Part two: the reveal against real vote data. The gap is the story. Per filmed gig.

**ONE HAS TO GO** — Attract. 10–15s. Elimination. Five staples, one disappears forever. Always live-performance framed. Fortnightly.

**ROOM REPORT** — Position, Convert. 20–40s or static card. Real data from one gig: voters, votes, most wanted, biggest surprise, top unmet request, crowd rating. Per gig once data exists.

**TRY THIS TONIGHT** — Convert. 15–30s. One concrete demonstration, one objection killed, one offer. Never a feature tour. Weekly at Stage B.

**REQUEST DENIED** — Nurture, Attract. 20–40s. Performer war stories. Excellent for creator collabs and for stitch bait. Fortnightly.
