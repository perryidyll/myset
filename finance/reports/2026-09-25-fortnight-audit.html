Ninety-five guessed.Forty-two measured.

How often a phone in the room asks the server for the board, per phone-hour — the one behaviour dial the whole server bill hangs on. Guessed on 5 September; read off Netlify's own meters on 25 September, seven nights at a time.

Fifteen real nights are on the model now. The room got a little smaller, the tips held at a dollar a head, and a night costs about three credits — three cents — whatever its size.

The whole audit on one card

  • Fifteen nights (was eight): 8.6 phones a night, 2.73 h, 2.1 votes a phone, 10 songs. The app was used at 15 of 22 published gigs.
  • Money holds at $1.04 a head over the eleven nights with readable takings — but three nights carry 85% of it and the median night is $0. Every card tip so far is $10; the first two $5 vote packs arrived.
  • A night costs ~3 credits by Netlify's own meters (2.3–3.8 across seven nights), nearly flat from 3 to 11 phones. The model at the old guess said 3.9; calibrated it says 2.8.
  • The screen-on dial is now measured: 42 ticks per phone-hour → 9.4% (was a 22% guess). Traffic per gig falls from 2.7¢ to 1.9¢; the arena drops from "at the edge" to "holds".
  • Two bills, this period: deploys 1,785 credits (94%), everything the rooms did 117. The plan's 1,000 credits ran out on 15 Sep; a $5.48 pack bought itself.
  • The bandwidth-counter method is fenced (decision 0089, INVARIANT 0ge): laid over the 14 September night, this morning's quiet pair reads 350–750 ticks a phone-hour — 8–18× the meters. Netlify's per-day meters are the instrument now.

Shipped in this pass

What changed in the model

  1. The meter methodThe tracker reads ticks per phone-hour and credits per night off Netlify's per-day counts — a gig day minus an empty day — copied into the credits log by hand with a clean-from date. It names the method it used, and the page shows the metered credits beside its own.
  2. The screen-on dial solves itselfWhen real shows are on, the dial is set from the measured ticks (9.4%) and says so; the 22% guess is gone from the defaults' meaning, not from the slider.
  3. The board weighs what it weighs3,040 bytes on the wire for the 79-song board — about 200 B plus 36 B a song, gzipped. The tracker's constant matches; the test would fail if they drifted.
  4. Two fences on the bandwidth methodA quiet pair counts only within 48 hours of a bracket and only if the pair itself is under six hours; a bracket over 24 hours is withheld. Every withheld bracket says why.
  5. The seed, the copy, the testsFifteen nights baked into the page; the room-money help, the "two bills" note and the assumptions note carry the fortnight's numbers; 97 engine checks (was 89) and the tracker's offline suite — which had been failing on main since the split — pass.

Falsified

What was wrong before

  • The bandwidth counter as a per-night instrument. This morning's quiet pair (mark #5 at 09:52, #6 an hour later — the first ever) laid over the 14 September bracket reads 350–750 ticks a phone-hour at 3,470 bytes a tick, depending on which background is believed (7.0 MB/h from the pair, or none): 8–18× what the request meter says. The pair itself was not clean — that hour held this audit's own reads of the store. The account's idle traffic in mid-September was ten times today's (the mirror moving clips), so a pair from another week measures nothing about that night. And the 15 → 25 September bracket held seven nights and one 488 MB day; with the pair's background over its 249 hours it reads negative. Hence the three fences — and the rule that the marks for a night are taken with nothing else running.
  • "A no-gig day is ~3 credits of compute and ~1 of requests" (14 Sep). It is 1.7 and 0.6 now that the mirror skips clips; the "no-gig" day used then carried hand tests and the first warm-door pings.
  • 2,175 bytes a board. True for a 40-song room on 11 September; every song in the library has been live to the room since 13 September, and the founder's board is 79 songs.
  • The tracker's offline test. It built its synthetic night at 2,530 bytes a tick and nobody had run it since the split changed what a tick weighs — it was red on main. Fixed, and the new rules ride on it.
Worth knowing

Flags

  • 14 September reads $22, not $20. Two $1 test tips from 15 September midday fell inside that night's money window, which runs until the next show starts. Two cents a head across the set; left as is — the window rule is right and production is read-only here.
  • 488 MB on 24 September with nobody in the room. The requests sat at the empty-day level, so it is not a gig; myset.vip carries 1.9 of the period's 2.2 GB, and that day's landing-page copy edits reloaded a page with 33 screenshots many times — the likeliest source, unproven. Netlify Analytics would name the URL.
  • Deploys buy the packs. Auto-recharge is on; ten more deploys and the next $5.48 pack buys itself. Deploys have slowed to under two a day — the pace, not the traffic, sets the bill.
  • The Studio share is the last big assumption in a night's cost (see above). The Studio polls every four seconds only while its Live tab is on screen and the show is live, so the founder's own habit decides it.
  • The per-head figure carries everything and rests on three nights. One more night like Sunday's, or one more dry week, moves it more than any server number will.
