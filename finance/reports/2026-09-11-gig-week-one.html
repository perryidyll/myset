Gig Week One

What the first real week of shows says — from every record the app kept, checked twice against the raw files — and what it does to the money model.

tl;dr

  1. Four real nights, 47 phones, 95 votes. Rooms were 7 to 18 phones (average 11), sets ran 2 to 4 hours, and each phone did about 2 votes. The app was used at 5 of the 11 gigs you published since 30 Aug.
  2. Nobody could pay for most of the week. The Stripe key was dead from just after 30 Aug until Tuesday evening. Sunday and Monday's rooms saw a "More votes" button that errored on tap. Wednesday's 18-phone room had a working checkout all night — and its takings have not been read yet.
  3. Then a second bug hid the money anyway. The archive asked Stripe with an empty options object, which the Stripe library rejects before it even sends the request. So every night since 2 Sep was filed as "money unknown", key or no key. Fixed today, with the Studio's Money tab and the request-hold paths that shared it.
  4. Server cost is not the bill. Deploys are. The four gigs cost about 18¢ of server for the week. The 216 production deploys in the last 30 days cost about $34 — essentially the whole Netlify bill. One deploy costs as much as three or four gigs.
  5. Bandwidth is video, not polls. Of the 336 MB used since 8 Sep, one to three views of your three 75 MB profile clips explain most of what the model could not. One clip view moves the meter as much as seven gigs of polling.
  6. The model moved. Rooms are bigger than the one-night seed (11 vs 8) but smaller than the guess (20). The opening scenario now shows $3,835 revenue and $2,165 profit a month at 1,000 artists, break-even at 148 artists (was 212). Every dollar of that move is "more phones × the same $0.375 a head" — and $0.375 is still one $3 sale on one night, measured per voter, not per phone.

1 · Which shows counted

You said to keep only the shows that lined up with gigs you had published. So the rule is now written into the tracker: a show counts if it started on a published gig day, no earlier than 90 minutes before the slot and no later than its end. A show started at any other hour is a test, however long it ran.

That rule sorted all 17 records without a single judgement call: 5 real nights, 2 gigs where the app was on but nobody used it, 1 accident, 10 hand-started tests. Plus 4 published gigs (31 Aug–3 Sep) that left no record because one show was left running for 108 hours across them.

2 · How rooms behave

Phones in the room vs phones voting
per night · phones present (dark) · most voting at one moment (light)
Votes per phone shrink as the room grows
votes ÷ phones present, per night
  • Four or five phones vote at any one moment, whatever the room size. So a bigger room does not vote more — it votes less per head: 2.3 votes a phone at 7 phones, 1.9 at 14, 1.5 at 18. (Sunday's 3.1 includes one phone that cast 5 votes in a single round — above the free allowance, so almost certainly a test phone or yours.)
  • Voting happens when people sit down. On the three comparable nights, 30% of all votes were cast before you logged the first song, and the last half hour of every set drew 0–2 votes. All four Monday requests came in the first 32 minutes.
  • About 3 in 10 votes never turn into a play (at least 28 of 95). The same songs keep getting asked for and skipped: Landslide (3 nights), All Of Me (3 nights), Ain't No Sunshine (2). A fan who backs one of those watches their vote sit on the board all night.
  • The room "chose" 41 of 45 song starts — but usually by one vote. Most winners were ties at 1–2 votes. Only once did you clearly skip a higher-voted song (Sweet Home Alabama over Free Fallin' on Wednesday). Four starts had no votes at all (2009 twice).
  • At least 40% of phones voted; probably 50–85%. The archive only keeps the busiest moment (a floor of 40%), but the 3-credit allowance means Monday needed at least 9 of 14 phones, Tuesday 6 of 7, Wednesday 9 of 18.
  • The free allowance is a hard ceiling. 3 credits per phone (5 until Tuesday night), one credit per vote, and a song request costs all 3. With payments down all week, nobody could go past it. The measured 2 votes a phone is already two-thirds of the ceiling.
  • Requests: 4 on one night, 1 in 4 played. Monday's four song requests came from four different phones; you added Wonderwall and declined the other three together 37–54 minutes later. No other night had any.

3 · The money

What is known: one $3 vote pack on 30 Aug. That is the only room money MySet has ever recorded — $0.375 per voter (8 voters that night; phones were not counted then).

What happened this week: the Stripe key stored on the server was dead from soon after 30 Aug. Sunday and Monday's rooms were shown "More votes · From $5" and "Tip", and a tap ended in "Card payments aren't working right now — grab Perry between songs." The key was replaced on Tuesday at 9:57 pm — after Tuesday's last song. So Wednesday at Anantara (18 phones, 27 votes) is the only night this week where a working checkout met a voting room. Whether anyone bought is unread: the last Stripe pull was Tuesday night, and the Stripe connector in this session needs reconnecting.

Fixed today — the bug that would have hidden it anyway. When a show ends, the archive asks Stripe for that night's payments. For an artist on the platform account (you) it passed an empty options object, and the Stripe library rejects that before sending anything. Every night since 2 Sep was filed as "unknown" because of it, and would have been forever. The same mistake sat in the Studio's Money tab, the books, and the path that releases a held request. One helper fixes all of them; the test double now rejects an empty options object exactly as the real library does, which is how 50 tests had missed it.

What the model would have expected: at $0.375 a head, this week's 47 phones would be about $18; per voter (19), about $7. The model's default $0.40 a head means roughly 8% of phones buying the $5 pack. Nothing this week can confirm or deny that.

Prices also changed mid-week: 5 free votes and $5 for 5 until Tuesday night; 3 free votes, $5 for 3 and $20 for 15 from Wednesday. Only Wednesday ran on today's economics.

  • Deploys are the bill. 24 production deploys since the billing period reset on 8 Sep = 360 credits ≈ $3.60. Bandwidth in the same days: 336 MB = 6.7 credits ≈ 7¢. Over the last 30 days: 216 deploys = 3,240 credits ≈ $34 on the Personal plan (the $9 plan plus five $5 top-ups). The four gigs: about 18¢. Shipping less often is worth 100× more than any polling optimisation. check Netlify's own usage counter could not be read from here — the billing page should show roughly 360 credits from deploys this period.
  • Bandwidth is clips. The model's own arithmetic puts the two real gigs in the period at ~56 MB and everything else it knows about at under 150 MB. The rest of the 336 MB is the size of one to three full views of your three profile clips (74, 75 and 79 MB — uploaded on or after 7 Sep, when the size cap was raised). One view is 1.5 credits, about a third of a whole gig.
  • Polls per phone-hour is still unmeasured. The only two bandwidth readings sit in different billing periods, so nothing brackets a night — and Sunday and Monday's gigs fell before the 8 Sep reset with no reading taken, so they can never be measured now. The screen-on dial stays a guess.
  • Netlify Blobs costs nothing on paper. There is no credit line for storage or reads; the store's cost shows up as function time (about 42 ms per read, ~15 reads per poll). All the text in the store is about 90 KB. The three clips are 227 MB — 2,500× everything else.

5 · What changed in the model

The tracker

  • Counts a night only when it lines up with a published gig; names every rejected record with its reason.
  • A night's length is the record — unless the 3-hour auto-end let it overrun the slot, then the later of the slot and the last song started.
  • Two records inside one gig (an accidental End then Start) merge into one night.
  • Lists published gigs that left no record at all, so "nights per week" is counted from the calendar, not the archive: 5 used of 11 published.
  • Measures votes + requests per phone and feeds the model's "actions per person" dial.
  • The after-gig bandwidth reading takes --clip-views N, because one clip view is 30,000 polls' worth of bytes.
  • Tested offline against this week's real records (25 checks) so the rules cannot drift without a test failing.

The page

  • Opens on the five measured nights unless you have switched real shows off. Seven dials now carry a "from 5 real shows" mark: people, length, actions per person, the three room-money dials, deploys.
  • A new line for profile clips watched per gig guess: 0.5 a gig at 75 MB each — the one line the bandwidth reading could not be explained without.
  • The Benchmark case reads the measured room instead of a hard-coded 8.

6 · How the data is stored

Netlify Blobs is not a database with tables. It is a shelf of named JSON documents: you ask for a document by its exact name, you get it back, you can put a new version in its place. There is no search and no "give me all the shows" — so every name has to be something the code can work out, and where a list is needed the code keeps its own index document.

  • One store, named "myset", shared by everything — including deploy previews. A preview reads and writes the same live data; only payments are fenced off (no key outside production).
  • Names are built from the artist's id. Your live show is one document. Your fans' votes are split across 12 documents by a hash of the phone's id, so a burst of votes spreads over 12 shelves instead of fighting over one. Your past nights are one document each, with an index that lists them. Your calendar, lyrics, charts, images and clips are separate documents. A second artist is simply a second set of names.
  • A night's record is written when the show ends or the next one starts, and always before the fans' vote records are wiped. It holds the song-by-song timeline with a snapshot of the top 8 songs on the board at each start, the counts (votes, phones present, wifi networks, busiest moment), and a cached copy of what Stripe said about that night's payments. About 3 KB a night.
  • Deliberately not kept: who voted for what. At the end of a night each fan's record is deleted or reduced to their unspent paid balance. No IP address is ever stored — only a short hash mixed with the artist id. So "did the same people come back?" is unanswerable by design.
  • Money lives in Stripe. The archive only caches a lookup; the ledger and the Money tab pull from Stripe each time.
  • Simultaneous writes are safe because every write says "only if the document is still the version I read" and retries up to 40 times if not. That is what makes 80 phones voting at once lose nothing.
  • Three ways in: the site's own functions; the Netlify command line signed in as you (which is how this analysis read everything, read-only); and the tracker script that wraps it.
  • Nothing expires and there is no backup. Netlify keeps it encrypted, but nobody has ever taken a copy. Today's read-only snapshot and the Google Sheet export are the only copies outside Netlify. About half the 199 documents in the store are leftovers from load tests and the pre-multi-artist layout; nothing reads them.

8 · Small things the data showed about the app

Noted, not fixed — none of them touch the model.

  • The archive's "requested but never played" list counts a vote once per round it sat on the board (one vote for Bar Song shows as 13). The Studio's history view shows that inflated number.
  • Every Start tap counts against a free artist's 4-night allowance — including tests and accidental restarts. Your show document counts 25 starts in September against 15 real ones. A free artist on your calendar would have been refused from the fifth start.
  • Archives do not record who started or ended a show; this week's start/end attribution came from millisecond timing. Copying that into the record would make next week's analysis a lookup.
  • The per-artist history index carries a later end time than the detail record for every show that was archived again at the next start (Monday reads 4.6 h in the index, 2.8 h in the record).
  • Six records still say "Ugly Duckling" as the venue because the show copied the profile's venue setting; the calendar match is the truth.
