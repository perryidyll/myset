Money model for MySet

Move a dial, watch the business. Every number on this page comes out of one formula: how many phones are in how many rooms for how long, what the room and the artists pay, what the server and Stripe take, and what is left. Nothing here is fixed — the guesses are marked, and real show numbers replace them as they arrive. One word to know: a tick is one phone quietly asking the server “anything new?” — not a vote. Since 11 September a tick is two small requests: the room’s shared board, and the phone’s own line. Ticks are what the server bill is made of.

How these numbers are made

What is measured, what is guessed, and the rules the engine keeps.

Does installing the page as an app change the server bill?

No, not today. The voting page asks the server the same way whether it is open in Safari or installed on the home screen: it walks the same 3 s → 10 s → 25 s ladder, it goes silent the moment the screen is off in both cases, and the app’s service worker never caches the live show — every ask still reaches a function. Installing changes nothing the server is charged for.

What would change the bill is replacing asking with telling — push instead of polling. That only works for an installed app on iPhone, it needs a permission prompt, and it fits the artist (who installs the Studio anyway) far better than a stranger in a bar. So the honest reasons to reward installing are retention, not cost: a home-screen icon brings people back, it opens faster, and it makes a future “your artist plays tonight” notification possible.

Three things worth knowing before nudging anyone to install: the voting page never actually asks (the install card lives on the city page, not the page the room is holding); on iPhone an installed app keeps its own storage, so a fan who installs mid-gig becomes a stranger and loses paid votes — ask after the show, never during; and installing cannot be checked by the server, so never attach money to it. Sound reasons to ask anyway: Safari wipes a site’s memory after seven days of not visiting, an installed app keeps it, and that memory is the fan’s credits and requests. The dial “Installed as an app” stays on the page so this is not forgotten; it deliberately moves no cost line.

Two bills, never one number

The server bill is two bills that have nothing to do with each other. The traffic bill is what the rooms cause — web requests, bandwidth, function compute — and it scales with gigs, phones and hours. The shipping bill is production deploys × 15 credits, and it scales with how often code is shipped and with nothing a room does. Three times a "server cost per gig" on this page or in a report quietly carried the deploys spread over the month's gigs, and polling looked dear when shipping was. On the week of the first four gigs, deploys were 98% of the credits and the gigs were 18¢.

So the rule, written into the engine and held by the test suite (INVARIANT 0fx): no per-gig, per-phone or per-show figure ever contains a deploy. The traffic bill is what the same month would cost with zero deploys; the shipping bill is what the deploys add on top. Turn the deploys dial from 0 to 400 and no per-gig number moves — the suite checks exactly that on every host. The tracker reports the two the same way: —. Netlify itself meters them separately (Usage & billing › Credit usage breakdown), which is where the exact split lives; each reading is kept in finance/credits.json and the tracker carries the latest one here. A day with no gig costs about 1.7 credits of compute, 0.6 of requests and 0.3 of bandwidth (Sat 19 Sep 2026: 3,200 requests, 13.7 MB — the scheduler, the warm-door pings, the nightly mirror), and a night with a room adds about 3 credits on top — 2.3 to 3.8 across the seven nights of 16–23 Sep, by the same meters.

If a big artist shows up: the tidal wave

Since 11 September the room shares one board. Every tick is two requests: the board — the same bytes for every phone, rendered about once per interval for the whole room and an edge hit for everyone else — and a small personal call that reads two documents instead of the whole crowd. So a poll no longer gets heavier as the room grows; the render does, and the room shares it. The server also sets the pace: 3 s between ticks up to 200 phones, 10 s to 3,000, 20 s above — and above 200 the tally moving no longer speeds anyone up. What stops a big room now is the document store, not the function count: the “Store MB/s” column in the size table is what it has to move to answer the room, and past ~50 MB/s the room stops working whatever the bill says (the repo’s simulator puts that wall near 2,500 phones at today’s record size). Pick “Netlify before the split” in the host list to see the old numbers: —.

What is built, and what is not.

  1. built The shared board (decision 0034). Its cost is in every number on this page; the “edge nodes” dial is the one thing about it nobody has measured — under a 10 s interval the board’s copy lives on each edge node separately, and a bar’s phones may land on one node or several.
  2. built The server sets the pace. The floor above is sent with every board; the “extra brake” dials on this page sit on top of it, for a room the founder wants slower still.
  3. built, off The open line (decision 0036). A Cloudflare object holds every phone’s socket and relays each landed vote as a dated hint, so the tally moves in a quarter-second instead of once per interval. Phones keep polling — the line is an add-on, and every failure falls back to the ladder. It is priced here from the probe’s own bill (two requests per socket, one per nudge, duration ≈ 0) as “Netlify + the open line”: — for the bar set. It costs more, not less — a hidden page closes its socket and a shown one reopens it, so every glance is a socket — and what it buys is a live tally, not a saving. It stays off until LINE_URL and LINE_KEY are set, and no real night has run it yet.
  4. to do Auto-recharge is on. Add a burn watchdog. At zero credits Netlify pauses every site on the account. A five-minute check of credits during a live show is the only real ceiling. Half a day.
  5. to do Sell the arena instead of absorbing it. A booked, prepaid tier above about 500 expected phones, priced off the “Charge to book” column. Policy in an hour, the form in a day or two.
  6. to do Terms that say so. Above 500 attendees needs a booking; MySet may slow the refresh, pause voting or cap participants. Two hours with a template.
  7. to do A kill switch. One flag that puts a room into read-only without a deploy. It has to stop the reads, not just the writes. Half a day.
  8. to do Move the store off the read wall. The bag of fan records is what the render moves; one small document per fan, or the tally kept in the object the line already has, is what lifts the ~2,500-phone ceiling. Days, after a booking pays for it.
  9. to do Edge rate limits per phone. Bounds a script or a bot, not a real crowd. An hour.

Every crowd figure above 18 phones is simulation from measured inputs; the biggest room a real night has held is 18.

What is measured, what is published, what is guessed

  • measured What a night really costs, by Netlify’s own meters (seven nights, 16–23 Sep 2026, each read against the one empty day of the same week): a room of 3–11 phones adds about 3,100 requests, 1.6 credits of compute and 37 MB over an empty day — about 3 credits, 2.3–3.8 across the seven — and the number barely moves with the room’s size, because the Studio’s own tick and the page loads are most of it. Read as ticks, with the Studio tab assumed on screen 60% of the night, that is ~40 ticks per phone-hour; the screen-on dial is solved from it (the 5 Sep guess of 22% gave 95). The method and the days are in tools/actuals.py and finance/credits.json; the bandwidth-counter method stays, but only for a bracket with a quiet pair of marks from the same day (decision 0089).
  • measured Bytes on the wire: the old poll 2,530 (production, 5 Sep); the board 2,175 for a 40-song room (11 Sep) and 3,040 for the founder’s 79-song board (25 Sep) — about 200 B plus 36 B a song, gzipped, and the room downloads it on every tick; the personal call 430 for a phone that voted, 208 for one that only watched; reads per call (15 per board render, 2 per personal call, 4 per vote, 21 per Studio poll — counted through the real code), 42 ms per document read; one fan’s record 101 bytes plus ~240 per cast. The 155 ms per render was a probe before a fix; the account’s own bill implies 70–110 ms, so 120 is the default here. The open line’s two requests per socket and 1.7 GB-s per 12,202 connections are the probe’s own bill (decision 0035).
  • estimated 50 ms per personal call (two parallel reads plus overhead — not yet measured in production); how many edge nodes a bar spans (the “edge nodes” dial, at its midpoint); the nudge’s round trip from Netlify’s region (115–140 ms from a Mac); reconnections and phones on the line at a real gig.
  • simulated The 20-phone, 3-hour gig (about 5,900 ticks, 4.8 credits on this page; the repo’s simulator says 5,821 ticks, 2,274 renders and 4.5 credits best case because it runs at 155 ms with no Studio and no extra tick per action — the test suite holds the two within 5%) is the polling ladder run against a model of how people glance at phones — reproducible, but the biggest real room so far is 18 phones. Every crowd figure above that is simulation from measured inputs.
  • published Netlify credit rates and plan prices; Cloudflare, Vercel rates; Stripe’s card, Billing, Connect and dispute fees — all read from the providers’ own pages on 5 Sep 2026 (links in the footer).
  • guess How many artists, on which plans, playing how often to how many people; what a room spends; how many gigs get promoted; fixed costs. These are the dials. Turn on “Use real shows” when the numbers exist.
  • Whose Stripe fee is whose. Tips and vote packs are charged on the artist’s connected account, so Stripe’s card fee on them is the artist’s cost, not MySet’s; MySet’s cut arrives with no extra Stripe fee on it. Subscriptions and featured shows are charged on MySet’s own account (a US account, checked 5 Sep 2026 — so US rates. Three dollars in four are on cards from abroad: Stripe’s own fees on the 12 room payments so far, $7.02 on $85, fit the US rates exactly at 75% international (the 5 Sep guess of one card in eight read the fees 17% low). Those are fans’ cards in Thai rooms; artists paying for plans may differ), so their card fee, the 0.7% Billing fee, disputes and refunds are MySet’s. Express account and payout fees are charged to the platform — and the code pays artists out daily, so a busy artist can cost 25¢ a night in payout fees; the dial defaults to 8 payouts a month and a weekly schedule would cut it. Uncollectable negative balances on artists’ accounts also land on MySet, hence the small bad-debt line.
  • Two things the code promises that the money does not yet do. The referral “free month” only moves a date in the registry; a paying referrer keeps being billed. And the founder’s own gigs cost server credits but produce no platform revenue. Neither is modelled as a separate line.
  • The free tier’s four-gig cap is applied: a free artist who wants six nights gets four. The refused nights are shown as a line in the formula.
