Money model for MySet

  • Move a dial, watch the business. Every number on this page comes out of one formula.
  • The formula: how many phones are in how many rooms for how long, what the room and the artists pay, what the server and Stripe take, and what is left.
  • Nothing here is fixed. Guesses are marked, and real show numbers replace them as they arrive.
  • A tick is one phone quietly asking the server “anything new?” It is not a vote.
  • Since 11 September a tick is two small requests: the room’s shared board, and the phone’s own line.
  • Ticks are what the server bill is made of.

How these numbers are made

What is measured, what is guessed, and the rules the engine keeps.

Does installing the page as an app change the server bill?

  • No, not today. The voting page asks the server the same way in Safari or on the home screen.
  • Same 3 s → 10 s → 25 s pace, silent when the screen is off, and the live show is never cached.
  • What would lower the bill is the server telling phones instead of phones asking (push). That needs an installed app and a permission prompt — a better fit for the artist than a stranger in a bar.
  • So the reasons to reward installing are coming back, not cost: a home-screen icon, a faster open, and future “your artist plays tonight” alerts.

Before nudging anyone to install

  • The voting page never asks — the install card lives on the city page.
  • On iPhone an installed app has its own memory. A fan who installs mid-gig loses their paid votes. Ask after the show, never during.
  • The server can’t check who installed, so never attach money to it.
  • A good reason anyway: Safari wipes a site’s memory after 7 days away, and that memory is the fan’s credits and requests. An installed app keeps it.
  • The “Installed as an app” dial stays on the page as a reminder. It moves no cost on purpose.

Two bills, never one number

  • The traffic bill is what the rooms cause: requests, bandwidth, compute. It grows with gigs, phones and hours.
  • The shipping bill is code updates (deploys) × 15 credits. It grows with how often code ships, and nothing a room does.
  • Mixing them made polling look expensive three times. The week of the first four gigs, deploys were 98% of the credits and the gigs cost 18¢.
  • The rule (INVARIANT 0fx): no per-gig, per-phone or per-show number ever includes a deploy. Turn the deploys dial from 0 to 400 and no per-gig number moves — the tests check this on every host.
  • Latest reading: —.
  • Netlify meters the two separately (Usage & billing › Credit usage breakdown). Each reading is saved and the latest shows here.
  • A day with no gig costs about 2.6 credits (scheduled jobs and checks). A night with a room adds about 3 credits — 2.3 to 3.8 across seven nights, 16–23 Sep 2026.

If a big artist shows up: the tidal wave

  • Since 11 September the room shares one board. Each tick is two requests: the shared board (same for every phone) and a tiny personal call.
  • So a phone’s check no longer gets heavier as the room grows. Building the board does, and the whole room shares that.
  • The server sets the pace: a tick every 3 s up to 200 phones, 10 s up to 3,000, 20 s above.
  • What stops a big room now is the database, not the bill. Past about 50 MB/s (“Store MB/s” in the table) the room stops working — near 2,500 phones today.
  • Pick “Netlify before the split” in the host list to see the old numbers: —.

What is built, and what is not

  • built The shared board (decision 0034). Its cost is in every number on this page; the “edge nodes” dial is the one thing about it nobody has measured — under a 10 s interval the board’s copy lives on each edge node separately, and a bar’s phones may land on one node or several.
  • built The server sets the pace. The floor above is sent with every board; the “extra brake” dials on this page sit on top of it, for a room the founder wants slower still.
  • built, off The open line (decision 0035). Moves the tally in a quarter-second instead of once per tick. Phones keep polling underneath, so any failure falls back safely. It costs more, not less: — for the bar set. It buys a live tally, not a saving. Off until its two keys are set; no real night has used it yet.
  • to do Auto-recharge is on. Add a burn watchdog. At zero credits Netlify pauses every site on the account. A five-minute check of credits during a live show is the only real ceiling. Half a day.
  • to do Sell the arena instead of absorbing it. A booked, prepaid tier above about 500 expected phones, priced off the “Charge to book” column. Policy in an hour, the form in a day or two.
  • to do Terms that say so. Above 500 attendees needs a booking; MySet may slow the refresh, pause voting or cap participants. Two hours with a template.
  • to do A kill switch. One flag that puts a room into read-only without a deploy. It has to stop the reads, not just the writes. Half a day.
  • to do Move the store off the read wall. The bag of fan records is what the render moves; one small document per fan, or the tally kept in the object the line already has, is what lifts the ~2,500-phone ceiling. Days, after a booking pays for it.
  • to do Edge rate limits per phone. Bounds a script or a bot, not a real crowd. An hour.

Every crowd figure above 18 phones is simulation from measured inputs; the biggest room a real night has held is 18.

What is measured, what is published, what is guessed

  • measured What a night really costs: about 3 credits (2.3–3.8 across seven nights, 16–23 Sep 2026) by Netlify’s own meters. It barely changes with room size, because the Studio and page loads are most of it. That works out to ~40 ticks per phone-hour (decision 0089).
  • measured Sizes and speeds: the board is 2,175 bytes for a 40-song room and 3,040 for 79 songs; a personal call 208–430 bytes; 42 ms a database read.
  • estimated 120 ms to build a board (a probe read 155; the bill implies 70–110); 50 ms per personal call; how many nearby servers a bar spans (“edge nodes”); open-line reconnects at a real gig.
  • simulated One gig at the dials: —, from a model of how people glance at phones. The tests hold it within 8% of the repo’s simulator. The biggest real room so far is 18 phones.
  • published Netlify, Cloudflare and Vercel rates; Stripe’s card, Billing, Connect and dispute fees. Read from their own pages, 5 Sep 2026, and checked again 1 Oct 2026 (links at the bottom).
  • guess How many artists, which plans, how many gigs and fans, what a room spends, fixed costs. These are the dials. Turn on “Use real shows” when the numbers exist.
  • Whose Stripe fee is whose:
    • Tips and vote packs are charged on the artist’s account, so that card fee is the artist’s. MySet’s cut arrives with no extra fee.
    • Plans and featured shows are charged on MySet’s own US account, so their card fee, the 0.7% Billing fee, disputes and refunds are MySet’s.
    • 3 of every 4 dollars come from cards abroad. Stripe’s real fees on the first 12 payments ($7.02 on $85) match US rates at 75% international.
    • Those were fans’ cards in Thai rooms. Artists paying for plans may differ.
    • Payout fees land on MySet. Paid plans are paid out daily, so a busy artist can cost 25¢ a night; Hobbyist is paid every Monday. The dial assumes 8 payouts a month.
    • Unpaid negative balances also land on MySet — hence the small bad-debt line.
  • Two promises the money does not keep yet: a referral “free month” moves a date but the referrer is still billed; and the founder’s own gigs cost server credits but earn no platform revenue. Neither has its own line.
  • The free plan is 10 shows in total, ever — not a monthly cap. A free artist plays the nights they want until the ten are used, then upgrades or stops. The formula shows how many months that lasts at the dials.
